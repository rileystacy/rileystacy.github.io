research: |
  I study fairness in computational systems and the law in order to keep AI systems accountable. Legal protections against discrimination, deception, and unfairness were written for *human* decision-makers—my research asks what those protections should mean when the decisions or falsehoods come from algorithms.

  My work spans two connected areas:

  - **Technical definitions of legal doctrine.** Showing that disparate impact and UDAP doctrines each require a different technical definition of a “less discriminatory alternative.”
  - **Generative AI, convergence, and decision making.** Examining diversity collapse in model-generated peer review and how AI disclaimers shape user reliance in high-stakes settings. When AI systems stand in for many independent human judgments, those judgments can become uniform and harmful.

  Across these areas, I combine empirical ML evaluation, user studies, and legal analysis to propose AI governance that holds up under both technical and legal terms.
